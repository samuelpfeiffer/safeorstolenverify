Why You Must Check Items Before Buying Secondhand

Buying stolen goods isn't just risky - it's illegal and costly. Here's what's at stake when you skip verification.

Legal Consequences

In most jurisdictions, knowingly or unknowingly purchasing stolen property is a criminal offense. Police can confiscate your purchase without compensating you, and you may face criminal charges including receiving stolen property (a misdemeanor or felony depending on value).

Real case: A Florida man spent $2,500 on an iPhone from Facebook Marketplace. Two months later, police seized it as stolen property. He lost both the phone and his money, plus faced criminal investigation.

Financial Loss

Americans lose $15 billion annually to secondhand marketplace fraud and stolen goods. When you buy a stolen item, you lose 100% of your money - no refunds, no recourse. Sellers of stolen goods disappear immediately after sale, and marketplaces rarely offer buyer protection for in-person transactions.

Average loss: $850 per victim for electronics, $3,200 for vehicles, according to FBI statistics.

How Criminals Exploit Online Marketplaces

Thieves target Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, OfferUp, and eBay because they can quickly offload stolen goods to unsuspecting buyers. 60% of stolen electronics are resold within 24 hours of theft. Professional theft rings steal hundreds of phones, laptops, and power tools daily, then list them at "too good to be true" prices.

Common tactic: Thieves use fake profiles, burner phones, and public meetup spots to avoid detection. They're gone before you realize the item is stolen.

Real Case Studies

Case 1: Texas woman bought MacBook Pro for $600 on OfferUp. Original owner tracked it via Find My Mac and called police. Buyer arrested, charged with receiving stolen property, lost $600 + legal fees.

Case 2: Construction company purchased $12,000 in "used" DeWalt power tools from Craigslist seller. All tools were stolen from job sites. Company lost tools, money, and faced insurance complications.

Case 3: College student bought iPhone for $400 on Facebook Marketplace. Phone worked fine for 2 weeks, then carrier blacklisted the IMEI as stolen. Phone became unusable brick - total loss.
