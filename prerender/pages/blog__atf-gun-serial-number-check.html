ATF Gun Serial Number Check — How to Verify Firearms Online

The ATF's eTrace system and the FBI's NCIC stolen gun file are both restricted to law enforcement — no consumer service can query them. Here's what you can check yourself, and how to close the gap before you buy.

Updated March 2026 · 7 min read

Checks the HotGunz national registry + serial integrity analysis

What is ATF eTrace?

The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) operates eTrace — an internet-based firearms tracing system used by over 8,000 law enforcement agencies across 46 countries. When a firearm is recovered at a crime scene, law enforcement uses eTrace to trace the gun's chain of custody from manufacturer to distributor to the first retail purchaser.

Here's the critical limitation: eTrace is not available to civilians. Only law enforcement agencies with approved access can submit trace requests. If you're a private buyer looking to verify a firearm before purchase, you cannot access eTrace directly.

SafeOrStolen cannot query eTrace either, and we won't pretend otherwise. What we do is check the serial number against the HotGunz national stolen-firearms registry (32,000+ user-reported records) and analyze it for signs of alteration or obliteration. That's a useful first filter for a private sale — not a substitute for a law enforcement check.

What SafeOrStolen Actually Checks

We'd rather be accurate than impressive. A firearm check on SafeOrStolen does exactly two things:

  • HotGunz National Stolen-Firearms Registry — A free public registry of 32,000+ firearms reported stolen by owners and theft victims, nationwide
  • Serial Number Integrity Analysis — We analyze the serial itself for obliterated, altered, or placeholder patterns — a defaced serial is a federal felony under 18 U.S.C. § 922(k)

We do not query ATF eTrace, FBI NCIC, or state police systems. Those are closed to consumer services by law. For a definitive answer, ask your local police non-emergency line to run the serial through NCIC — and if you're buying through an FFL, dealers have been able to voluntarily query the NCIC Stolen Gun File since August 2025.

Does the ATF Keep a Gun Registry?

No. Federal law (the Firearm Owners' Protection Act of 1986) explicitly prohibits the ATF from establishing a national firearms registry. The ATF cannot maintain a searchable database of gun owners or gun sales. Instead, ATF traces work by sequentially contacting manufacturers, distributors, and dealers — a manual process that can take days or weeks.

For consumers, the practical question isn't where a gun came from — it's whether it's been reported stolen. SafeOrStolen answers a narrow version of that question instantly by checking the HotGunz registry, then points you to the police check that answers it completely.

What is ATF eTrace?

ATF eTrace is the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives' internet-based firearms tracing system used by 8,000+ law enforcement agencies. It traces firearms from manufacturer to first retail sale.

How do I verify a firearm online for free?

Enter the serial at SafeOrStolen.com. We check the HotGunz national stolen-firearms registry (32,000+ records) and analyze the serial for alteration or obliteration, in about 3 seconds. 2 free checks, no credit card.

What's the difference between ATF eTrace and FBI NCIC?

eTrace traces a gun's sales history; NCIC records whether it's been reported stolen. Both are law-enforcement-only. SafeOrStolen checks neither — it checks the HotGunz registry and serial integrity.
