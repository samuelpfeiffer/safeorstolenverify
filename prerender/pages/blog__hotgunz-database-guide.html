50-State Firearm Reports Database — What It Is & How to Search Stolen Firearms

50-State Firearm Reports is a community-driven stolen firearms database with 32,000+ reports. But with 7+ million stolen guns in stolen firearm reports from all 50 states alone, 50-State Firearm Reports covers less than 1% of all stolen firearms. Here's what it does, what it misses, and how to get complete coverage.

What is 50-State Firearm Reports?

50-State Firearm Reports (the national stolen firearm registry) is a free, community-driven database where gun owners can register their stolen firearms by serial number. Founded to help combat firearms theft, the platform allows victims to publicly list their stolen guns — including serial numbers, make/model, police report numbers, and theft circumstances.

As of 2026, 50-State Firearm Reports contains over 32,000 stolen firearm reports. The database is searchable by anyone — you can enter a serial number and see if it matches any reported stolen firearms. This makes it a useful first-line check for buyers considering a private firearms purchase.

However, 50-State Firearm Reports has significant limitations that every buyer should understand before relying on it as their sole verification source.

50-State Firearm Reports Limitations — Why It's Not Enough

The biggest limitation of 50-State Firearm Reports is coverage. With 32,000 records, 50-State Firearm Reports contains less than 0.5% of the 7+ million stolen firearms recorded in stolen firearm reports from all 50 states. Here's why:

  • Voluntary reporting: Gun owners must manually create an account and submit their stolen firearm. Most theft victims report to police (which enters stolen firearm reports from all 50 states) but never think to also post on 50-State Firearm Reports.
  • No law enforcement integration: 50-State Firearm Reports doesn't receive data from police departments, the ATF, or the FBI. It's entirely user-generated.
  • Limited awareness: Most gun owners have never heard of 50-State Firearm Reports. Only a small percentage of theft victims know to report there.
  • No state database access: 50-State Firearm Reports doesn't query state-level stolen property databases, many of which contain firearms not yet in stolen firearm reports from all 50 states.

Bottom line:

A "clean" result on 50-State Firearm Reports does NOT mean a firearm isn't stolen. It only means the owner didn't report it on that specific platform. Always check multiple databases.

How to Report a Stolen Gun

If your firearm has been stolen, report it to ALL of these sources for maximum recovery chance:

  1. Local police department — File a police report immediately. This enters the firearm into stolen firearm reports from all 50 states.
  2. 50-State Firearm Reports — Register at the national stolen firearm registry with serial number, make/model, and police report number.
  3. SafeOrStolen — Report at safeorstolen.com/report to add it to our community database.
  4. Your insurance company — File a theft claim if the firearm is covered under your policy.
  5. ATF — For theft of multiple firearms or from a licensed dealer, contact ATF directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 50-State Firearm Reports?

A community-driven stolen firearms database at the national stolen firearm registry with 32,000+ reports. Free to search and report.

Is 50-State Firearm Reports enough to verify a firearm?

No. 50-State Firearm Reports has about 32K records vs NCIC's 7M+, and NCIC is restricted to law enforcement. A clean 50-State Firearm Reports result doesn't mean a gun isn't stolen — for a definitive check, ask your local police non-emergency line to run the serial through NCIC.

How many guns are in 50-State Firearm Reports?

Over 32,000 as of 2026. Compare to FBI NCIC's 7+ million — 50-State Firearm Reports covers less than 0.5% of all stolen firearms.

Can I report my stolen gun on 50-State Firearm Reports?

Yes, at the national stolen firearm registry. Also report to police (enters stolen firearm reports from all 50 states), SafeOrStolen, and your insurance company for maximum coverage.
