iCloud Locked Phone: What It Means for a Used-iPhone Buyer

By Samuel PfeifferLast reviewed February 8, 2026

You power on a used iPhone and instead of the Hello screen you get a login box asking for an Apple ID that isn't yours. That's Activation Lock — Apple's anti-theft feature. It's the number-one reason "cheap" used iPhones on Marketplace are cheap.

An iCloud-locked phone isn't legally proof of theft, but in a used-buyer context it almost always points that way. Here is what Activation Lock actually is, what it's worth, and what to check before you hand anyone money.

What Activation Lock (iCloud Lock) actually is

When Find My iPhone is turned on, Apple ties the device to the owner's Apple ID. Anytime the phone is erased or restored, it demands that Apple ID password before it will finish setup. There is no factory reset that beats it. There is no DFU trick. There is no jailbreak that removes it on any modern iPhone. Without the original owner's password, the phone is a paperweight — a beautiful, expensive paperweight.

Apple built it this way on purpose, after a wave of iPhone thefts in the early 2010s. It works. iPhone thefts dropped sharply once Activation Lock rolled out, because a stolen iPhone with no way to reset became worth almost nothing on the resale market.

The three reasons a used iPhone might be locked

  1. Legitimate mistake. The seller forgot to sign out. Rare, but it happens. Fixable in five minutes if they can log into iCloud.com and remove the device from Find My.
  2. Estate / gift / lost password. The original owner is unavailable (passed away, upgraded and lost the password). Sometimes recoverable through Apple with proof of purchase — usually not.
  3. Stolen. The seller doesn't have the password because the account isn't theirs. This is the majority of "cheap iCloud locked" listings.

The pricing tells you which one it is. A legitimately locked iPhone gets sold at near-market price with the seller present to unlock it. An iPhone listed at 30% of retail "as-is, iCloud locked" is almost never case #1.

How to check for Activation Lock before you buy

The seller needs to do this in front of you, at the meetup:

  1. Ask the seller to open Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Erase All Content and Settings.
  2. When it asks for the Apple ID password, they enter it and the phone completes the erase.
  3. The phone reboots to the Hello screen — no Activation Lock. That is a genuinely clean sale.

If the seller offers to hand you the phone already reset "so you can set it up fresh", that's the exact scenario where Activation Lock appears. You want them to erase it in front of you — not before you arrive.

Apple used to run a public Activation Lock status checker page. They retired it. The only reliable pre-purchase check is now the on-device erase above, plus an IMEI check on our phone check to catch a matching stolen report.

iCloud locked vs blacklisted vs stolen

These are three different systems and they don't always overlap.

  • Activation Lock: Apple's software lock, tied to Apple ID. Prevents setup.
  • Carrier blacklist: Network-level block, tied to IMEI. Prevents cellular service. See what a blacklisted IMEI is.
  • Reported stolen: A police or community stolen-item report tied to the serial or IMEI.

A used iPhone can be any combination of the three. iCloud-locked and not blacklisted is common (owner enabled Find My but never reported it to the carrier). Blacklisted and not iCloud-locked is possible too (owner didn't have Find My on but did file with the carrier). Ideally, you check all three before you buy.

Can you unlock an iCloud-locked iPhone?

Only the original account holder can, from iCloud.com under Find My. Every service advertising "iCloud removal" for money falls into one of three buckets: outright scam that takes your money and disappears; phishing service that tries to trick the original owner into handing over their password; or an ex-Apple-employee insider service that ends when Apple audits the account. None of them are things you want to be involved with.

What to do if the phone you already bought is locked

The playbook is the same as any suspected stolen phone: don't reset it (the lock screen is your evidence), document the seller and the payment, and read our step-by-step for what to do after buying a stolen phone. If you paid with a credit card or PayPal Goods & Services, open a dispute the same day.
