What an IMEI is (and how to find it)

An IMEI — International Mobile Equipment Identity — is a permanent 15-digit serial number unique to a specific handset. Unlike a SIM or phone number, it stays with the physical device for life, which is exactly why stolen-device databases like the GSMA registry are built around it.

Three ways to find a phone's IMEI:

  • Dial *#06# on the phone's keypad — the IMEI appears instantly.
  • Settings → About phone (Android) or Settings → General → About (iPhone).
  • Scan the barcode on the box or SIM tray with the SafeOrStolen app — no typing.

When buying used, always read the IMEI off the actual device with *#06# — not off a box or a screenshot a seller sends you.

What your result means

Clean

No reported-stolen or blacklist flags found. The phone isn't in the records checked. Still confirm the seller can factory-reset it and that it isn't locked to an account (iCloud Activation Lock / Google account).

Reported stolen

The IMEI has been reported stolen or lost. Do not buy it. Buying a device you know is reported stolen can cost you both the phone and your money — the exact situation SafeOrStolen exists to prevent.

Blacklisted

A carrier has flagged the IMEI in the GSMA registry. The phone may work today but can be blocked from networks at any time. Treat a blacklisted result as a hard stop on any purchase.

"Stolen" vs "blacklisted" — what's the difference?

Reported stolen means an owner or the police flagged the device as taken. Blacklisted means a carrier will deny it service. A phone can be blacklisted for a stolen report, for unpaid bills, or for fraud — so a blacklist check catches problems a stolen-only lookup misses. SafeOrStolen looks at both in one result.
