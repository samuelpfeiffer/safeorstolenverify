Florida Laws on Stolen Vehicles

Under Florida Statutes § 812.014, grand theft auto is a third-degree felony (up to 5 years prison, $5,000 fine). Under § 812.019, dealing in stolen property is a second-degree felony (up to 15 years). Possessing a stolen vehicle, even unknowingly, results in confiscation.

Florida Vehicle Market & Theft Overview

Florida's vehicle market is the third-largest in the nation with over 17 million registered vehicles. Miami-Dade County alone rivals many states in vehicle registrations, while Orlando, Tampa, and Jacksonville each represent major markets. Florida's status as a retirement destination means the market includes many low-mileage vehicles from snowbirds. However, Florida also faces unique challenges: hurricane damage, salt-air corrosion in coastal areas, and a transient population that makes ownership verification complex. The state's ports—Miami, Jacksonville, and Tampa—serve as both import and export hubs, adding international dimensions to the market. Florida's no-inspection policy means vehicles with mechanical issues may circulate freely.

Understanding Florida Vehicle Theft Patterns

Florida ranks among the top 10 states for vehicle theft with Miami-Dade and Broward counties leading the state. Miami's major port enables stolen vehicles to be shipped to Caribbean islands, Central America, and South America before they're even reported missing. Professional theft rings operate sophisticated export operations targeting luxury vehicles and SUVs. Hurricane seasons create additional problems: flood-damaged vehicles are totaled by insurance, then title-washed through other states and resold in Florida with clean titles. The state's tourist population provides cover for thieves, and rental car fraud is common. Jacksonville and Orlando also see significant theft activity, often tied to I-95 and I-4 corridor operations.

Florida VIN Check Tips

  • Always check for flood/salvage title brands via NMVTIS, especially after hurricane seasons
  • Inspect for water damage signs: musty smell, water lines, rust under carpets
  • Verify FLHSMV title has proper security features and formatting
  • Be cautious of vehicles from Miami-Dade and Broward counties without clear history
  • Check for salt corrosion on coastal Florida vehicles

Expert VIN Verification Advice
