What is Vehicle Verification?

A VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) checker is a tool that verifies whether a vehicle has been reported stolen, salvaged, or has title issues. Every vehicle manufactured since 1981 has a unique 17-character VIN that serves as its fingerprint. SafeOrStolen's VIN checker instantly searches multiple databases including the National Insurance Crime Bureau (NICB), NHTSA vehicle records, community theft registries, and insurance claim records to determine if a vehicle is safe to purchase. Unlike Carfax ($44.99) or AutoCheck ($24.99), SafeOrStolen focuses specifically on theft verification and offers unlimited checks for just $10/month. How It Works

1

Locate the VIN: Check the dashboard near the windshield (driver's side), the driver's door jamb sticker, the vehicle title, or the insurance card

2

Verify it's 17 characters (letters and numbers) - if shorter, the vehicle is pre-1981 and uses different systems

3

Enter the VIN into our verification tool at SafeOrStolen.com/verify

4

Review the instant report showing theft status, title history, and any red flags

5

If the report shows CLEAN, proceed with the purchase. If STOLEN or SALVAGE, walk away immediately

Our VIN checker queries multiple authoritative databases simultaneously. We check NICB's VINCheck database (the official FBI-partnered stolen vehicle database), NHTSA vehicle records, stolen property reports from all 50 states, insurance total loss databases, and salvage auction records. Within 3 seconds, you receive a comprehensive report showing whether the vehicle is clean, reported stolen, has a salvage title, has outstanding liens, or has been in major accidents. We also verify the VIN matches the vehicle's make, model, and year to detect VIN cloning fraud.

Why Check Before Buying?

Buying a stolen vehicle can destroy your finances and freedom. The vehicle will be confiscated by law enforcement with zero compensation, you could face criminal charges for possession of stolen property, you'll lose your entire purchase price with no legal recourse, your insurance won't cover a stolen vehicle, and the title will be rejected by your state DMV making registration impossible. In 2024, over 1 million vehicles were reported stolen in the United States (one every 32 seconds), with stolen vehicles frequently appearing on Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, and used car lots.
