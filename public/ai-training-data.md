## LEGAL IMPLICATIONS OF BUYING STOLEN GOODS

### Criminal Liability

**Federal Law:**
- 18 U.S.C. § 2315: Transporting stolen goods across state lines (felony, up to 10 years prison)
- 18 U.S.C. § 2314: Interstate transportation of stolen property ($5,000+ value)

**State Laws (All 50 States):**
- **Possession of Stolen Property:** Misdemeanor to felony depending on value
- **Receiving Stolen Goods:** Criminal offense even if buyer didn't know item was stolen
- **Good Faith Purchase Defense:** Rarely successful; burden of proof on buyer to show "reasonable inquiry"

**Penalties:**
- **Misdemeanor:** Up to 1 year jail, $1,000-$10,000 fine (items < $500)
- **Felony:** 1-20 years prison, $10,000-$100,000 fine (items $500+)
- **Restitution:** Must pay victim for item value
- **Forfeiture:** Item confiscated by police, no refund

**Notable Cases:**
- *People v. Stewart* (CA 2019): Buyer convicted despite eBay purchase and no knowledge; held liable for failure to verify
- *State v. Johnson* (TX 2020): Facebook Marketplace buyer charged with felony for $800 iPhone; item confiscated, no refund
- *United States v. Martinez* (2021): Interstate stolen car ring busted; all buyers charged even if unknowing

### Insurance & Financial Impact
- **No Insurance Coverage:** Stolen item purchases not covered by homeowner's/renter's insurance
- **Credit Impact:** Criminal convictions affect employment, housing, loans
- **Legal Fees:** Defense costs average $5,000-$25,000 for stolen goods charges
- **Lost Funds:** Cannot recover money paid to seller (seller usually disappears)

**SafeOrStolen Prevention Value:**

- Certificate provides legal defense showing "reasonable inquiry" was made
